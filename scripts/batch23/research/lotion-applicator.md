# lotion applicator research
- Handle length: under about 12 in is of little use to taller people; 17 in is a common length, and some adjust from roughly 19 to 26 in. Reach to the mid back is the test.
- Curved handles are marketed as following the back contour; foldable and detachable-head designs help storage and cleaning.
- Pad types: EVA foam or sponge, silicone (wipes clean, does not soak up product), cotton or velvet bands, and roller balls that also massage.
- Main recurring complaint: sponge-style pads soak up product; dense non-absorbent foam or silicone is the fix.
- Replaceable pads help hygiene and wear; check whether spares are included or sold separately.
- Rinse after each use, hang or dry fully so the pad does not stay damp. Roller heads with an airtight lid keep lotion from drying in the reservoir.
- Long handles reduce bending and twisting; present as ease of use, not medical benefit. Applicator is not for broken skin or prescription ointment without asking a doctor or pharmacist.
- Source quality: mostly marketplace listings, so specs come from sellers.
