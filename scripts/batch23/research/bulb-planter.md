# bulb planter research
- Long handled planters let you work standing, helpful for back/knee comfort and large borders.
- Hand planters are compact, cheaper, suit small jobs and containers.
- Long-handled models struggle in hard clay and compacted soil; plugs may need manual removal.
- Plant in soil loosened or moistened; rock-hard summer soil defeats most planters.
- Depth-marked heads, soil release, rust-resistant finish are key features. Depth consistency matters most.
- Tulips in heavy slow-draining clay: stick to about 6 inches, amend with compost.
- Bulb rule of thumb: plant about 2-3 times the bulb height; check the packet for each bulb.
- Thin or hollow shafts bend in heavy soil; reinforced steel and a solid foot plate matter.
- Drill-powered augers need a 3/8 inch chuck, cordless drill with good torque; wider bits stall low-voltage drills.
- Augers throw soil, wear eye protection; avoid drilling near buried lines.
- Keep augers clean and dry to prevent rust.
## clay additions
- Grit or coarse horticultural sand in the hole helps drainage; avoid fine play sand, it can set like concrete in clay.
- Depth advice in clay: rule is about 3x bulb diameter; heavy soils often planted 1-2 inches shallower to avoid rot.
- Tulips, alliums, hyacinths need well drained soil; snowdrops and winter aconites tolerate damp clay.
- Amending the whole bed is often ineffective if clay lies just below the worked layer; a grit pocket per bulb can help.
- Not verified: effect of augers on hole walls in clay; do not claim.
