# shoelace research
- Length: match the eyelet count or measure the old laces. One retailer chart pairs 3-4 eyelet pairs with about 60 cm, 5-6 pairs with 80 cm, 6-7 pairs with 100 cm. Charts vary, so measure when possible.
- Flat laces are the usual choice for sneakers and leather dress shoes and are said to lie neatly under the tongue; round laces suit boots and are commonly described as more abrasion resistant but sometimes less secure to knot.
- Oval (half round) laces are described as a middle ground: low profile like flat, hold like round; popular on running and sports shoes.
- Polyester and nylon are common durable materials; cotton is soft but frays sooner; waxed laces suit dress shoes; rope laces are thick and seen on hiking and work boots.
- Width matters: about 5 mm is slim, about 7 to 8 mm is a bolder flat lace.
- Elastic no tie laces: thread through eyelets, adjust tension, lock with an anchor, clip or buckle, test the fit, and trim last. Leave a small tail (a couple of centimetres) so the lock holds without a long loose end.
- Anchors and locks keep elastic laces at the chosen tension; some designs use metal buckles or tips, others silicone.
- Elastic laces are one size and can be trimmed; if between lengths, listings differ on going shorter or longer, so test before cutting.
- Sources were retailer pages and product guides; treat durability claims as general, not settled.
