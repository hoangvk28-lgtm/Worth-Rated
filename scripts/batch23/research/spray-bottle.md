# spray bottle research
- HDPE is the usual recommendation for bleach and harsh cleaners; PET compatibility with bleach is disputed. Diluted vinegar is more forgiving in either plastic. Check the maker's chemical list.
- A bottle labeled bleach resistant should be HDPE; the weak link is usually the trigger and nozzle internals, not the bottle. Glass ball valve and stainless spring resist corrosion better than all-plastic internals.
- Adjustable nozzle with mist, stream and off settings is standard; the off setting reduces leaks in storage.
- Ergonomic or multi-finger triggers are marketed to reduce hand fatigue; claims are vendor claims, not independent tests.
- Output per pull: one vendor reports more than three cubic centimeters per pull on a 32 oz industrial model; no cross-brand data.
- Amber glass blocks UV that can degrade essential oils; glass does not absorb oils like some plastics; glass is heavier and breakable; plastic is lighter and cheaper in multipacks.
- Never mix bleach with ammonia or vinegar; label every bottle with contents and date; do not refill food or drink bottles.
- Sources were retail and blog pages; treat as general guidance.
