# emergency light research
- Brightness: roughly 20-40 lumens is enough to navigate a hallway or bathroom; a whole room feels lit at about 80 lumens or more. Lumens measure total light; beam distance is a separate matter.
- Runtime: look at stated runtime at each brightness level; peak output drains batteries fastest. Plan for 6-12 hours on a low or medium setting for a typical outage.
- Layered setup: automatic plug-in lights cover the first seconds after a failure, handheld lights or lanterns cover movement and rooms.
- Place plug-in units on the route to the bathroom, near stairs, hallways and bedrooms; they should switch on automatically when mains power drops.
- Bulb-style emergency bulbs: check socket type (E26), shade clearance and whether the bulb works as a normal bulb with the switch on.
- Battery type (NiMH vs lithium-ion) and charge hold matter; test units periodically and recharge on a schedule.
- Check whether the light keeps charging while plugged in and whether it doubles as a night light or flashlight.
- Marketing claims (lumens, runtime) are the maker's claims; do not treat any as code-compliant exit lighting unless the listing says so.
