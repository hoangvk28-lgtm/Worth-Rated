# oven mitt research
- Silicone mitts are commonly rated roughly 400-500F; plain cotton is often cited nearer 350F and suits quick, lower-temperature tasks. Listing ratings vary, so read each product's own number.
- Cotton quilting is more flexible; silicone is waterproof and stain resistant but can restrict movement. Hybrids pair a silicone grip print or shell with a cotton lining.
- Cuff should extend past the wrist; longer sleeves protect forearms from rack edges.
- Thick, dry insulation matters. Wet cloth conducts heat fast, so a damp cotton mitt protects far less.
- Synthetic fillers such as neoprene or standard polyester wadding can melt or degrade under high heat; check the lining.
- Thickness trades protection for dexterity; pot holders cover flat contact, mitts cover grabbing.
- Steam from lids and pots is a common burn source; longer cuff and waterproof outer help (inference, not sourced).
- Sources were retailer pages and ratings disagree, so treat temperatures as listing claims.
