# splatter screen research
- Fine stainless mesh vents steam with little restriction (crisper searing and frying); silicone is easier to clean but can trap more steam. Perforated steel resists clogging but blocks more steam.
- Finer mesh catches more grease but can clog with grease and food particles; vendor claims such as 95 percent oil blocked are unverified marketing.
- Size to the largest pan; 13 inches is the common universal size. A screen only works if it covers the pan.
- Handle: check the material and heat rating; plastic handles can melt near a hot rim; folding metal handles store flat. One silicone listing cited a limit of about 445 F (230 C); verify per listing.
- Care: grease polymerizes in mesh over time; soak in hot water and baking soda. Screens with silicone or aluminum rims are often hand-wash; plain stainless is usually dishwasher safe.
- Sources were retail and review pages; treat specifics as general guidance.
